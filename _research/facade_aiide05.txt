Evaluating Agency 
The structure of narrative content in

Local Agency When the players actions cause immediate, context-specific, meaningful reactions from the system, we 
call this 
local agency
. Furthermore, the greater the range of actions 
the player can take, that is, the more expressive t

interface, then the richer the local agency (again,

Façade

such as navigation, picking up objects, hugging and
 
kissing. The millions of potential player inputs a
re 
mapped, using hundreds of authored

topic 
references, and explanations. Another set of rules

context proposers

specific ways, such as agreement, disagreement, all

or provocation (
Mateas and Stern 2004b
). 
 Ideally there would be immediate, meaningful, cont
ext-
specific responses available at all times for all D

actual implementation of 
Façade

ideal is reached ~25% of the time, where the player

satisfying degree of real-time control over Grace a

emotional state, affinity to the player, which topi
c is being 
debated, what information is being revealed, and th

current tension level. But more often, ~40% of the

only a partial ideal is reached: the mapping/interp

ore 
generic and/or not as immediate. Furthermore, ~25% 
of the

clusters, but also have the potential to shift on a

by-moment basis. There are two main reasons for these varying level

local agency. First, from a design perspective, at

progress. Second, and more often the case, a lack 
of local 
agency is due to limitations in how much narrative 
content 
was authored (see the Failures section below). Global Agency The player has 
global agency

Façade

the
